EXPLORE DRIFT

Ride a trail. Follow a clue. Hear a place differently.

DRIFT is a map for curious walks and rides, with clues to music and creative finds around Hines Park. A drift is an outing led by curiosity: explore a new trail, linger by the water, and notice the sounds, light, and details you usually pass. Come for a small treasure hunt or a reason to get outside; hear place-linked sounds where the landscape becomes part of the experience.

A REALLY TRAILS ONLY MAP

TRAILS ONLY opens with trail lines on a quiet background. Switch to STREET MAP for streets, or turn on signals, landmarks, and city labels under MAP LAYERS.

RESET MAP returns to the park. PLACES stays visible for quick navigation. Tap a trail or marker for details; on a computer, you can also hover.

YOUR TRAIL COLLECTION

Browse Hines MTB routes, paved paths, and published access points in TRAILS. Save favorites on this device, show available routes on the map, or open access directions in Apple Maps.

Some routes have guide links rather than drawn lines. Use the MCMBA Hines guide and trail conditions when planning a ride.

WHAT IS A PSYCHOGEOCACHE?

In DRIFT, a psychogeocache is a creative find tied to a place: a sound, transmission, or clue that invites you to explore and notice your surroundings.

Psychogeography explores how places influence feelings and behavior. Curiosity, atmosphere, and unexpected details can guide a drift. Explore the idea at CU Boulder ↗

Geocaching contributes the search: follow locations and clues, find something, and record the discovery. Traditional caches usually include a physical container and logbook. Learn the basics at Geocaching 101 ↗

DRIFT combines them: a clue brings you somewhere, then the sounds, light, history, and mood become part of the encounter. The find can be a transmission rather than a box.

“Psychogeocache” describes DRIFT’s approach here. DRIFT is an independent project.

FIND FIELD FINDS

Enable FINDS under MAP LAYERS, then tap a marker to open its clue and guidance. Choose written clues, a map reference, or celestial guidance when a precise location is available.

With location enabled, nearby signals become discoverable by proximity. Record an intercept when you reach one. The Signal glyph opens the Loudest Riot signal portal.

WHEN YOU FIND A DRIFT CARD

A DRIFT NFC card is an optional doorway into the project, not a requirement for using the map. You might find one that opens a place-linked sound, a clue, or a project page. Its destination depends on the link programmed onto that particular card. Not every map marker has a physical card.

NFC is Near Field Communication. It is designed to work only a few centimetres away, so hold the top edge of an iPhone almost against the card for a second or two. Move slowly across the card if it does not read immediately. On supported iPhones, a notification or Safari prompt appears; tap it to continue.

Scan out of curiosity: the card may connect the place you are standing to something to hear or explore. Scanning opens a link; it does not automatically verify your GPS location, save a find, or share anything. Leave the card where you found it unless it is clearly marked to take.

RECORD YOUR DRIFT

RECORD ROUTE records a breadcrumb path using precise location fixes. SAVE ROUTE keeps the walk in SAVED, where you can reopen it on the map.

Saved walks, favorites, and personal intercepts stay on this device. Sharing an intercept to the public field log is optional.

SAVED includes a device-local nickname and EXPORT MY DATA. Export your personal finds, favorites, and route summaries as JSON. Exact route coordinates are excluded unless you choose to include them. There is no account sync or import yet.

LOCATION ON YOUR TERMS

Explore without granting location. Tap LOCATE when you want it. Approximate fixes never recenter the map; recording and proximity discovery use precise fixes.

The app can retain its trail reference map for offline use after a successful online load. Street tiles and external links need a connection.
