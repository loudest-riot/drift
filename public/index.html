WHAT IS A PSYCHOGEOCACHE?

In DRIFT, a psychogeocache is a place-linked creative find: a sound, transmission, or clue that invites you to explore and pay attention to where you are.

Psychogeography explores how places shape our feelings and behavior. Drifting means letting curiosity, atmosphere, and unexpected details influence the route.

Geocaching supplies the hunt: use a location and clues to find something, then record the discovery. Traditional geocaches usually contain a physical logbook in a hidden container.

The connection: follow a clue to a place, then notice its sounds, light, history, and mood. DRIFT’s find can be a transmission rather than a box; the encounter with the place is part of the find.

DRIFT // FIELD SIGNAL
DISTRIBUTED RELAYS IN FIELD TERRAIN

A little adventure in Hines Park.

DRIFT is a map for curious walks and rides, with clues to music and creative finds around Hines Park.

A drift is an outing led by curiosity: take a different turn, linger by the water, and notice the sounds, light, and details you usually pass.

Come for a new trail, a small treasure hunt, or a reason to get outside. Hear nøfuture’s place-linked sounds where the landscape becomes part of the experience. You can simply explore; recording your route is optional. Landmarks are named places; waypoints are cool spots, rocks, plants, geological features, or wildlife sightings you notice. Keep dated observations in your private JOURNAL.

  1. Pick somewhere to explore. Open TRAILS for routes and access points, or PLACES for a park landmark.
  2. Follow a clue. Turn on FINDS in MAP OPTIONS, then tap a marker for its clue. LOCATE can show where you are.
  3. Discover the sound. A clue may lead to music or a creative find. You might also encounter a DRIFT NFC card: scanning one could open a place-linked sound, a clue, or a project page. The destination depends on that card. Cards are optional; one is not promised at every marker.

ENTER THE FIELD opens the map. RECORD ROUTE saves the path of your walk or ride to JOURNAL. Location is optional for browsing; recording and nearby-find checks need precise location.

Your routes and personal finds stay on this device. Sharing a find is optional.
