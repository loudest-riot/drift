WHAT IS A PSYCHOGEOCACHE?

In DRIFT, a psychogeocache is a place-linked creative find: a sound, transmission, or clue that invites you to explore and pay attention to where you are.

Psychogeography explores how places shape our feelings and behavior. Drifting means letting curiosity, atmosphere, and unexpected details influence the route.

Geocaching supplies the hunt: use a location and clues to find something, then record the discovery. Traditional geocaches usually contain a physical logbook in a hidden container.

The connection: follow a clue to a place, then notice its sounds, light, history, and mood. DRIFT’s find can be a transmission rather than a box; the encounter with the place is part of the find.

DRIFT // QUICK START

DRIFT turns Hines Park into a field of hidden transmissions.

A psychogeocache combines geocaching’s search with psychogeography’s attention to place. In DRIFT, discover a transmission and notice what the surroundings make you feel.

MINIMAL shows trails on a quiet background. STANDARD adds streets. Open TRAILS for rides and access points. Choose HINES PLACES to explore. Hover a marker, or tap it on your phone, for details. Signal details include written clues and sky guidance.

LOOK FOR A DRIFT CARD. GPS gets you close to the field signal. When you find the physical card, hold the top of your phone almost against it for a second or two and scan its NFC chip. The scan opens the full transmission and confirms you found the right artifact. NFC is short range on purpose, so “right next to the phone” is normal. If nothing appears, move slowly across the card with the top edge of the phone.

Tap ◎ only when you want location. Approximate location never recenters the map. The Signal glyph opens go.loudestriot.com.

Your path and intercepts can stay on your device. Sharing is optional. Explore all DRIFT features →

FIELD RULE: stay on public land, obey closures, respect cemeteries and memorial sites, and never place a cache at a burial site or sensitive historic marker.
