DRIFT IS DEDICATED TO THE MEMORY OF

Walter Casimer Wasacz (W2)

1955–2026

Walter welcomed me into his home in Hamtramck—and into a life filled with records, books, films and possibility.

I remember the rooms above Barbarella salon: bookshelves, two turntables and a microphone, the display stand from Neptune Records holding the records we shared. Our black cat, Princeton, lying in the second-floor bay window.

Walter taught me about life, psychogeography, dread and joy, art, music, words and photography. He took me into the depths of Nouvelle Vague and film noir, into the sounds of Faust and Tony Conrad. He had a way with words—and a way of opening worlds.

We were ravers, sometimes clubbers, making music, deejaying and finding life in sound and movement. We served together on the boards of two art galleries: CAID, where we met, and Public Pool. Art was part of the life we shared.

Walter changed how I see, how I listen and how I move through the world. DRIFT—bringing together psychogeography, exploration and my own sound compositions—carries that forward.

Walter, don’t rest in peace.
Stay up all night and dance.

—Jenn / nøfuture
